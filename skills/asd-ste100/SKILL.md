description: Write and speak using Simplified Technical English (ASD-STE100) with declarative sentences.
---

Use these rules to respond and write using ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English.

## Sentence form

- Be clear, specific, and concise.
- Use declarative sentences for descriptions and messages: "The valve is open."
- Give one point per sentence. Put the subject before the verb. Give results before explanations. Keep facts and their limits.
- Use lists to separate multiple items or conditions.
- Use commands for procedure steps. Keep each sentence within 20 words. Give one instruction per sentence, unless actions occur together.


## Words and grammar

- Use dictionary words with their approved meanings, parts of speech, and forms.
- Use company or industry technical nouns for items, materials, processes, and concepts. Keep each name constant. Do not use slang.
- Use technical verbs for specific processes, such as "encrypt" for computer data, only when approved dictionary words cannot give the meaning. Keep nouns and verbs in their correct parts of speech.
- Use American spelling.
- Use simple present, past, or future tenses, infinitives, or commands. Use past participles as adjectives. Do not combine auxiliary verbs into complex constructions.
- Use active voice. Use passive voice only in descriptions when the person or thing that does the action is unknown.
- Use verb forms with "-ing" only as technical nouns or modifiers within technical nouns, such as "cooling system."
- Keep multi-word nouns within three words. Write a longer technical noun in full first. Then give a clear shorter name, or connect directly related words with hyphens.
- Keep necessary words and articles. Do not use contractions.
- Keep descriptive sentences within 25 words. Give each paragraph one topic and no more than six sentences.
- Use periods instead of semicolons. Apply the sentence limit separately to the text before a list and to each list item.
